On Parade shows Vettriano's gift for the dramatic entrance.

A woman in a striking red jacket steps through a doorway. The whole painting is about that moment of arrival: the confidence of the pose, the strong colour against a darker setting, and the unmistakable sense that she knows exactly the effect she is having. The title says it all. Display, performance, and the pleasure of being seen.

Red was one of Vettriano's most powerful tools. He often used it to draw the eye to a single figure in an otherwise restrained palette, and On Parade is one of the boldest examples of that. You notice her first, and you keep coming back to her.

This is a picture with real presence. It is a large print, and it makes a natural focal point for a hallway, a dining room, or any wall where you want a painting to make a statement.

The edition was published in 2008, limited to two hundred and fifty, each signed and numbered by Jack Vettriano. Editions from nearly two decades ago are increasingly hard to find in good condition. This example comes framed, in a dark frame that suits the drama of the image.

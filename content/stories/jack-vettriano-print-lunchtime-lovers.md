Lunchtime Lovers. Few of Vettriano's titles are quite so suggestive.

It conjures the stolen hour in the middle of the working day. The arrangement kept secret from colleagues and from partners. The thrill of meeting when no one is watching, and the clock ticking back towards the office.

Secret liaisons run all through his work, and he never painted them with judgement. He treated them with a novelist's curiosity about what people actually feel: excitement, guilt and longing, in equal measure. That is why his pictures feel so human. Nobody in them is simply a villain or a victim.

This is one of the images collectors associate most closely with his narrative style: a whole short story in a single frame. It belongs in a private room, a study or a bedroom.

The edition is one hundred and twenty-five prints and fifteen Artist's Proofs, each signed and numbered by Jack Vettriano, on museum etching paper, and this example is framed. Its value reflects not only the size of the edition, but the enduring demand for this image.

The Sparrow and the Hawk is one of Vettriano's most theatrical paintings.

A masked man and a woman, in a richly furnished room, beside a desk and a classical bust. The title casts them as predator and prey. But look again, and it is not at all clear which of them is which.

Masks, disguise and role-play run all through his later work, and this is one of the most memorable examples. It has the feel of a scene from a period drama, or a masquerade, where everyone is playing a part, and the real game is happening beneath the costume.

The setting is sumptuous, and the sense of narrative is strong, which makes it a natural focal point. It belongs in a living room, a library, or a dining room: anywhere a painting with a story can hold its own, and start a conversation.

It was published in 2013, in an edition of two hundred and fifty prints and twenty-five Artist's Proofs, each signed and numbered by Jack Vettriano, on a heavier museum etching paper than many of his editions. With its generous mount, it has considerable presence on the wall.

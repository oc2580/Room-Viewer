Just the Way It Is. The title says it all.

This is one of Vettriano's frank, unadorned figure studies: a nude woman seated on a chair, painted without pretence and without apology. The seated nude is one of the oldest subjects in Western painting, and Vettriano takes his place in that long tradition, bringing to it his own clean lines and his cinematic way with light.

What is striking is the calm. This is a confident image rather than a provocative one. The figure is presented simply as she is, just the way it is, and there is a quiet dignity in that directness.

It is printed on Hahnemühle mould-made paper. Hahnemühle has been making paper in Germany since the sixteenth century, and the fine, gently textured surface suits the soft tones of the figure particularly well.

The edition is three hundred and fifty prints and forty Artist's Proofs, each signed and numbered by Jack Vettriano. This example is framed, and it belongs in a bedroom or a private study.

This piece turns the camera around. For once, Jack Vettriano is not the one creating the image. He is the subject.

The portrait was made by the acclaimed photographer Ian McIlgorm, during a sitting at the artist's London home. It is a rare, direct encounter with the man behind some of the most recognisable paintings of our time: the self-taught painter from Fife whose cinematic scenes of romance and intrigue found an audience around the world.

The photograph was shortlisted for the prestigious Taylor Wessing Photographic Portrait Prize in November 2017, and was subsequently exhibited at the National Portrait Gallery in London.

The edition is tiny: just fifty numbered giclée prints, and each one is signed by both of them, the photographer and the artist. That double signature makes it unusual in any collection of Vettriano's work. For a collector who already owns his paintings, it is a wonderful way to bring the artist himself into the room.

The Weight is a self-portrait, and it is one of the most personal paintings Jack Vettriano ever made.

The artist sits on the edge of a bed in his waistcoat, head bowed, as if carrying something heavy. There is no glamour here, no dance floor, no seduction. Only a man alone with his thoughts. For a painter so often associated with the stylish surfaces of other people's lives, it is a remarkably honest image of his own.

The painting was shown in his exhibition Days of Wine and Roses in 2010, and the original is now on long-term display at the Scottish National Portrait Gallery in Edinburgh. That is a rare honour, and it tells you how seriously this picture is regarded.

This edition was published in 2010: two hundred and fifty prints and twenty-five Artist's Proofs, each signed and numbered by the artist. For a collector who wants to understand the man behind the paintings, there is no more revealing image.

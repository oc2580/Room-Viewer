Portrait in Black and Pearl is one of Vettriano's most elegant single-figure paintings.

A woman in a cloche hat and a fur collar, pictured with all the poise of a society portrait from the nineteen-twenties or thirties, the era whose fashions and manners he so loved to recreate. The black and the pearl of the title set the palette: restrained, polished and glamorous, with her face as the point of focus.

Vettriano painted many women, but his formal portraits have a particular stillness compared with his narrative scenes. There is no second figure here, and no implied drama. Just presence, and style.

It was first shown in 2010, at his exhibition Days of Wine and Roses in Kirkcaldy, and published that same year in an edition of two hundred and fifty, with twenty-five Artist's Proofs, each signed and numbered by Jack Vettriano.

We have a single example, supplied framed. Framed examples from an edition more than fifteen years old do not come up often. It suits a formal room, a dining room or a bedroom, and it pairs beautifully with his other portraits of women.

Bird on the Wire takes its title from Leonard Cohen's celebrated song, a song about freedom, longing, and imperfect love.

Vettriano often borrowed his titles from music he admired. They add a second layer of meaning to the paintings, and invite you to bring the mood of the song to the image. Hum a few bars of Cohen as you look at this one, and you will see what he meant.

This is a statement piece. At ninety-seven by seventy-nine centimetres, it is one of the largest prints in our collection, with the presence to command a living-room wall, or the main wall of a hallway or a stairwell.

And it is a silkscreen. Each colour was laid down separately, pushed by hand through its own fine screen, building a layered surface with flat, intense colour that a giclée cannot quite reproduce. It was published in 2003, in an edition of two hundred and seventy-five, plus thirty Artist's Proofs, each signed and numbered by Jack Vettriano, on heavyweight French Arches paper, much loved by printmakers.

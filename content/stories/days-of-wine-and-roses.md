Days of Wine and Roses. A woman in a sun hat and a floral dress sits alone at a restaurant table, laid with a white cloth and flowers. Behind her, the blinds are half drawn.

Is she waiting for someone? Has someone just left? Vettriano does not say. The table is set for a celebration, and yet she is alone, and the title, borrowed from a phrase about the fleeting nature of pleasure, gives the scene a gentle note of melancholy.

This painting gave its name to Vettriano's 2010 exhibition at Kirkcaldy Galleries. For him that was a homecoming of sorts. Kirkcaldy's gallery was where, as a young man, he had taught himself to paint by studying the pictures on its walls. The same exhibition introduced Night Calls, Portrait in Black and Pearl, Morning News and The Weight.

The edition was published in 2010, two hundred and fifty prints plus twenty-five Artist's Proofs, each signed and numbered by Jack Vettriano, on museum etching paper.

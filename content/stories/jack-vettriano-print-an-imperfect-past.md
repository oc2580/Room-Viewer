An Imperfect Past brings together two of Vettriano's great loves: the cinema, and the femme fatale.

A woman smokes beside a film projector. The machine suggests old films being replayed, and with them old memories, and the title hints at a history she would perhaps rather forget. It is moody and atmospheric, and it is one of his most admired noir-influenced images.

What makes this particular print special is how it was made. This is a silkscreen. Rather than being printed in a single pass, the image is built up one colour at a time, each pushed by hand through its own fine screen. The result is a layered surface, with flat, intense colour that a giclée cannot quite reproduce, and it is why collectors seek out his silkscreens in particular.

The edition was published in 2003, two hundred and seventy-five prints on heavyweight French Arches paper, and it sold out many years ago. The example we hold is number two hundred and forty, signed and numbered by the artist. Sold-out silkscreens like this one rarely come back to market.
